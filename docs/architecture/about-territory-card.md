# Territory card: About

Status: **architecture approved 2026-08-31**. The public title and final framing of the founding-commitment/activism chapter remain open. Copy, claims, artifacts, and visualization remain subject to later review.

## Root verdict

**Retain About as a root territory with four substantive topics and no required internal cluster.**

Visitor question: **Why does LearnGraph exist, what commitments guide it, who is responsible for it, and through which real relationships does it develop?**

## Candidate topics

### Why LearnGraph

- **Current path:** `/about/why-learngraph`
- **Purpose:** State the educational thesis: material alone is not access; orientation, visible relationships, support, and agency make knowledge usable.
- **Likely form:** concise position article + one visual contrast + links to Platform mechanics
- **Boundary:** do not repeat the product explanation or claim improved outcomes without evidence

### Founding commitment / activism chapter — public label open

- **Working path:** unresolved until the topic's exact public job is clear
- **Purpose:** Address learning that is obstructed, restricted, unsafe, unequal, or politically constrained; explain why this work matters to LearnGraph; and give relevant activist groups, foundations, educational/cultural organisations, and implementation partners a serious entry point.
- **Likely form:** commitment statement + operational principles + proposed application artifacts
- **Owner truth recorded 2026-08-31:** fighting injustice and imbalance where possible is one motivation behind LearnGraph. The ten-person team shares a broad and intentionally non-uniform political orientation, shaped by different backgrounds, experiences, and emphases. It should not be converted into a collective identity label or a polished declaration of ideological sameness.
- **Operational meaning:** each initiative should state the conditions it actually requires—such as safety, facilitation, translation, connectivity, funding, cultural context, or trusted local leadership—rather than projecting one universal programme onto all work.
- **Boundary:** proposed work remains proposed until implementation and evidence exist.
- **Architecture reason:** `Why LearnGraph` can acknowledge this founding motivation, but cannot carry its depth without becoming a catch-all. The chapter also serves a distinct external audience and collaboration function.
- **Primary cross-link:** Collaborate / LearnGraph partnerships, because the chapter should provide a path from shared concern to possible funded or implemented work.

### People

- **Working path:** `/about/people`
- **Purpose:** Identify the current people responsible for LearnGraph and the expertise or contribution each person can factually claim.
- **Likely form:** compact roster; name, role, one factual contribution line, optional approved first-person sentence
- **Boundary:** historical contributors, current team, advisers, and partners must not be blended

### Network

- **Current path:** `/about/network`
- **Purpose:** Give honest context for LearnGraph's institutional and collaborative relationships using explicit relationship types.
- **Likely form:** categorized relationship directory + short verified notes + linked artifacts
- **Boundary:** logos are context, not endorsement, adoption evidence, or cases

## Remove or redistribute

- Contact leaves the editorial graph and becomes one canonical utility action.
- Legal entity details remain on the legal page.
- Product mechanics link to Platform.
- Funding, pilot, and implementation pathways link to Collaborate.
- Cases attach to the claims they substantiate rather than being duplicated in Network.

## Reusable artifacts

- current team roster and approved attribution
- verified partner/relationship entries
- ITECH as separate relationship and case objects
- Protected Learning Maps as a proposed application shaped by the access commitment
- dated, defined network figures only if they can be substantiated

## Owner decisions required

1. Which mission language is enduring organizational truth?
2. Which concrete situations and project types belong in this chapter without turning it into an unlimited social-impact category?
3. Which people are currently part of LearnGraph, in what role, and with what publication consent?
4. What is the precise current relationship with every named organisation?
5. Which cases and project roles may be attributed publicly?
6. What public label carries the chapter's real job without summarising the team's politics or reducing it to a slogan?

## Working recommendation

Remove Contact as an editorial topic. Retain the fourth chapter as an addressable home for the founding commitment, restricted/unequal learning contexts, relevant initiatives, and the collaboration path they may open; its public label remains unresolved. Rename Team to People unless organizational language later requires otherwise. Keep Network factual and relationship-typed.
