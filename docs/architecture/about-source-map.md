# About — factual source map

Status: source analysis for architecture work. This is not final copy, a final node list, or a topology decision.

## Scope and evidence rules

This map separates five different jobs currently mixed under `About`:

1. why LearnGraph exists
2. learning access / commitment
3. people
4. network
5. contact as a utility

Source status is explicit throughout:

- **implemented/public-site source** — present in the current or legacy frontend; still requires currentness review
- **internal position** — a developed statement of intent or principle, not proof of impact
- **proposal** — planned work, expected result, or conditional capability; never describe as achieved
- **unverified claim** — needs an owner, date, artifact, permission, or external evidence before publication

## Territory purpose to test

Give visitors the human and institutional context for LearnGraph: the problem it chooses to address, the commitments guiding that work, the people responsible for it, and the relationships through which it is tested. It should not become a container for product mechanics, project sales material, legal disclosures, or unsupported impact language.

## 1. Why LearnGraph

### Source-backed substance

- The Project Presentation frames the founding belief as freely accessible knowledge made usable through orientation, connection, support, and a continuing path. It describes LearnGraph as the structural layer that makes relationships, gaps, progress, and next steps visible. **Status: internal position.**
- The same presentation distinguishes a person's actual landscape of strengths, gaps, interests, and pressure points from flattened grades, certificates, and completion records. **Status: internal problem framing.**
- `HomeLanding.tsx` and `home.*` frame the broader mission as regaining collective agency and using technology without trading away dignity or independence. The page is currently unreachable according to the legacy inventory. **Status: implemented but orphaned framing; owner must decide whether it remains canonical.**
- The current `about/why-learngraph.ts` condenses the purpose into orientation without surrendering agency, understandable and adaptable paths, and a graph that is structural rather than decorative. **Status: current scaffold, not independently evidenced.**
- Human-centred and anti-black-box material recurs in the school, labour-market, individual, sovereignty, and service sources. It supports an enduring principle, but the audience-specific repetitions should not all migrate here.

### Possible content forms

- concise position article or manifesto
- one visual contrast: score/catalogue versus navigable landscape
- links to Platform / model and Platform / personal paths for implementation detail
- a short provenance note or founder statement only if authorship and current ownership are clear

### Cases or evidence that could support it

- `Inside LearnGraph` in the Project Presentation says a first release exists and describes its current experience. This can show that the idea became tangible only after product-owner verification of each present-tense capability.
- ITECH and Bildungszentrum Optimum material may illustrate why orientation and visible paths matter, but must attach to the precise claims they evidence rather than serve as generic mission proof.
- The Afghan pilot documents show how the principle has informed a concrete **proposal design**. They do not establish that the pilot ran or produced impact.

### Meaningful cross-links

- Platform / the model — how the graph turns the principle into structure
- Platform / personal paths — how orientation becomes an individual path
- Platform / evidence — how progress can become visible without collapsing into a score
- Platform / sovereignty — how agency and institutional control might be protected technically
- Research / questions — the unresolved technical and social questions behind the position

### Duplicate risks

- repeating the Project Presentation's learning-path explanation instead of linking to Platform
- merging dignity, privacy, open core, and self-hosting into one broad moral claim
- using audience pain points from `/schools`, `/labour-market`, and `/individual` as three versions of the same origin story
- treating the orphaned collective-agency wording as settled organization truth without owner confirmation

### Owner verification

- Is “knowledge should be freely accessible” an enduring organizational commitment, a product/business-model promise, or presentation language?
- Is “regain collective agency” still the preferred mission wording?
- Which current product facts may be used to demonstrate that the principle is implemented now?
- Who owns and may authorize any origin/founder account?

### Gaps

- no dated origin account or named author in the reviewed sources
- no concise distinction yet between LearnGraph's educational thesis and its product proposition
- no verified evidence yet for any claim that orientation has improved learner outcomes

## 2. Learning access / commitment

### Source-backed substance

- Project Presentation pages `Where Potential Gets Buried` and `Learning Access Under Unequal Conditions` argue that direction is part of access and that resources alone do not create usable opportunity. They name interrupted education, poverty, migration, language barriers, unsafe contexts, and restrictions on who may learn. **Status: internal position, not an achieved impact claim.**
- The presentation proposes that foundations and educational initiatives could use LearnGraph to connect community-specific paths, resources, skills, progress, privacy, and trusted support. Every “can” or “could” must remain conditional until implemented and evidenced.
- The Afghan Women Project corpus is a project proposal for `Protected Learning Maps`. It specifies trusted access, small groups, adaptable visibility, facilitation, mentor input, practical access support, protected documentation, and evaluation. **Status: proposal.**
- The proposal deliberately treats visibility as a choice and distinguishes private, internal, pseudonymous, anonymised, and restricted outputs. This is strong design material but not evidence that the platform currently implements every control described.
- Expected outputs and outcomes in document 08, sustainability claims in document 09, and the “tested project structure” in document 11 are all future-oriented. They must never be recast as completed participation, stronger orientation, documented progress, peer connection, dignity, or a validated model.

### Possible content forms

- a commitment statement defining what “access” includes beyond content availability
- a principles block: orientation, continuity, safety, privacy, trusted support, local adaptation
- a clearly labelled proposed-pilot brief or downloadable project concept
- an evidence area that remains empty or says “planned evaluation” until results exist
- later, a case study only after implementation, consent, safeguarding review, and documented outcomes

### Cases or evidence that could support it

- Afghan `Protected Learning Maps` is a **designed application/proposal**, not a case study.
- Bildungszentrum Optimum source material may support the problem of continuity and systematic personalisation if the relationship and observations are verified.
- Partner/network relationships concerned with inclusion or digital learning may supply context, but alignment with another organization's mission is not evidence of LearnGraph impact.

### Meaningful cross-links

- Collaborate / educational partnerships or protected pilots — implementation pathway
- Platform / personal paths — orientation and continuation
- Platform / evidence — documenting progress without defaulting to formal grades
- Platform / sovereignty — privacy, visibility, hosting, and control questions
- Research / questions — safety, portability, human agency, and evaluation
- About / network — trusted access and delivery relationships

### Duplicate risks

- absorbing the entire Afghan proposal into an About article
- presenting the proposal's program design as a generally available platform feature set
- repeating “dignity,” “opportunity,” and “human-centred” without explaining operational commitments
- turning a commitment into a campaign page or implying beneficiaries and results that do not yet exist
- duplicating collaboration/funding language from `Join the Map`, which belongs with partnership routes

### Owner verification

- Has any Afghan pilot been funded, contracted, begun, completed, or evaluated? If not, label it only as proposed work.
- Which privacy, pseudonymity, access-control, translation, connectivity, and protected-output capabilities exist in the product now, and which require project adaptation?
- Are the Afghan context references current, sufficiently specific, and cleared for public use? The proposal's bibliography is only a source list, not a full citation record.
- What public commitment can LearnGraph sustain regardless of whether this particular pilot proceeds?
- Who owns safeguarding, consent, data protection, and risk review for sensitive-context work?

### Gaps

- no completed pilot evidence, participant data, evaluation, or outcomes in the reviewed corpus
- cohort size, partners, duration, roles, access support, and funding remain explicitly unconfirmed
- no public safeguarding or ethical-participation framework
- no verified boundary between current platform controls and adaptations budgeted in the proposal
- no agreed measurement language for access, orientation, continuity, or dignity

## 3. People

### Source-backed substance

- `LandingTeamSection.tsx` contains ten named people; `en.json` supplies roles and first-person/value-oriented quotes. **Status: implemented legacy roster; currentness, consent, spelling, and quote ownership unverified.**
- The current `about/team.ts` describes combined experience across product, architecture, education, infrastructure, and public-interest collaboration. **Status: current scaffold; broad capability statement without named provenance.**
- Legacy service material describes workflow, architecture, implementation, critical-infrastructure, and European interoperability experience. These can support individual or team capability only when the responsible people, projects, roles, permissions, and dates are identified.
- The legacy-content DOCX recommends factual entries: name, role, one short expertise/contribution line, and at most one approved sentence in the person's own voice.

### Possible content forms

- compact current roster
- role/expertise cards with optional links to work they can publicly claim
- a small “how disciplines meet” explanation
- later, authorship metadata on articles or research artifacts

### Cases or evidence that could support it

- named, permissioned project roles in ITECH, critical infrastructure, or interoperability work
- repository contribution history where individuals consent to that linkage
- authored talks, papers, documentation, or public project outputs

### Meaningful cross-links

- Collaborate offers — delivery responsibility and relevant expertise
- Research / questions and contribution — authorship, stewardship, and maintainership
- About / network — keep people and institutions distinct

### Duplicate risks

- using personal values quotes as evidence of organizational capability
- copying the service trust section into team biographies
- presenting all historical contributors as current employees or team members
- creating long biographies where a role and contribution would be clearer

### Owner verification

- current roster, employment/affiliation type, titles, name spelling, preferred identity, ordering, and visibility
- consent for names, roles, photographs, quotations, biographies, and project attribution
- which experience belongs to LearnGraph as an organization versus to individuals from earlier work
- who currently owns product, research, educational, design, safeguarding, and partnership responsibilities

### Gaps

- no dates or affiliation types
- no approved short biographies or headshots in the reviewed source set
- no explicit maintainers, authors, or responsibility map
- no provenance for broad multidisciplinary capability claims

## 4. Network

### Source-backed substance

- The legacy landing page renders seven organizations with names, logos, and links: ITECH, Win4SMEs CoVE, All Digital, EGINA, POLITEHNICA București, FETICO, and Nebrija. **Status: implemented legacy roster; relationship status and permission unverified.**
- `partnersData.ts` also contains hidden `problem`, `solution`, and `learngraphRole` narratives. They are not rendered and are broad promotional assertions. **Status: internal/unpublished claims; medium-low confidence until verified with each organization.**
- The legacy scale bar states 20 institutions, 200 learners, and 8 countries. It gives no date, definition, denominator, or citation. **Status: unverified metrics; do not publish as evidence yet.**
- The current `about/network.ts` correctly frames partnership as contexts where vocabulary, pilots, and interoperability can be tested, while stating that cases belong beside the claims they support. **Status: architectural principle, not proof that every listed relationship does those things.**
- The Afghan proposal says implementation depends on trusted partner structures, but partners are explicitly unconfirmed. **Status: proposed relationship model, not network evidence.**

### Possible content forms

- categorized relationship directory with explicit labels such as partner, project participant, customer, funder, research collaborator, or network affiliation
- brief verified relationship notes and links
- map or domain grouping only if it clarifies real relationships
- attached cases/evidence blocks located with the claims they substantiate
- dated network metrics with definitions, if retained

### Cases or evidence that could support it

- ITECH may become both a verified relationship entry and a case, but those are separate content objects.
- Win4SMEs or other named projects may support interoperability/collaboration claims if formal role and outputs are documented.
- Bildungszentrum Optimum is source material for a possible case but is not in the rendered partner roster; its relationship type requires verification.

### Meaningful cross-links

- Platform / evidence — cases and measured use
- Collaborate / partnerships and pilots — ways relationships begin
- Research / interoperability — project outputs or standards work, when inspectable
- Research / contribute — contribution pathways distinct from commercial contact
- About / people — individual affiliations where relevant

### Duplicate risks

- logo wall presented as endorsement or proof
- counting institutions, projects, users, countries, and informal contacts as one undifferentiated “network”
- copying hidden partner narratives without partner approval
- duplicating cases here instead of attaching them to product, service, or research claims
- confusing participation in a consortium or memorandum with adoption of LearnGraph

### Owner verification

- current relationship and permitted public label for every organization
- logo/name/link permissions and approval of descriptive wording
- whether ITECH, Win4SMEs, All Digital, EGINA, POLITEHNICA București, FETICO, and Nebrija are current and publishable
- definition, measurement date, and evidence for 20 institutions, 200 learners, and 8 countries
- whether any named relationship is a customer, funded project, pilot, memorandum participant, informal collaborator, or aspiration
- publication status and evidence for ITECH and Bildungszentrum Optimum cases

### Gaps

- no dates, agreement references, public outputs, or contact owners attached to partner records
- no relationship taxonomy
- no verified adoption/evaluation evidence attached to the network list
- no confirmed access partner for the Afghan proposal

## 5. Contact — utility, not editorial evidence

### Source-backed substance

- The current graph gives Contact a full About topic with a mailto action.
- The legacy site repeats email, booking, footer, service, and legal contact routes.
- The topology pressure test identifies Contact as a utility action rather than a substantive editorial topic.

### Possible content forms

- persistent utility action in the site shell
- compact contact panel with route choices such as product, project, research, press, or legal only if those inboxes and owners exist
- one canonical email/contact route reused by relevant nodes
- legal entity and statutory contact kept on the legal page, not folded into About

### Cases/evidence and cross-links

- Contact needs no case or evidence.
- Relevant calls to action can link into the same utility from Collaborate, Research / contribute, About / network, and protected-pilot material.

### Duplicate risks

- multiple scheduling links and email addresses drifting out of sync
- turning “bring us a problem” sales copy into an About article
- mixing statutory contact, general conversation, project intake, and contribution workflow

### Owner verification

- canonical public email, booking URL, response ownership, spam/privacy handling, and available inquiry routes
- whether `contact@learngraph.org` is current
- whether research contribution should use email, GitHub, or another workflow

### Gaps

- no contact governance or routing information
- no verified canonical relationship between footer, service, imprint, and graph contact details

## Cross-cutting verification queue

Before any About material becomes final copy, owners should resolve:

- mission language and the boundary between principle, promise, and measurable impact
- current platform facts that can substantiate “why” claims
- exact status of the Afghan proposal and all sensitive-context capabilities
- current people, consent, roles, and attributable experience
- current network relationships, permissions, metrics, and cases
- one canonical contact route and separate legal disclosures
