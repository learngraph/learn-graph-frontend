# LearnGraph Graph Website Launch Roadmap

Status: active working roadmap

Timebox: four focused production days

The objective is not to complete every planned territory. The objective is to launch a graph in which every available click earns the next one.

## Working rules

- A designed node is not automatically launch-ready.
- Editorial approval, factual approval and implementation state remain separate.
- Unfinished work stays in the repository without appearing in the public graph.
- Real interfaces, artifacts and outcomes take priority over additional prose.
- A reserved territory is not a failure. A weak published click is.
- No node is added merely to make the topology look complete.

## Launch definition

The launch candidate is ready when:

- every visible territory and topic has content worth opening
- reserved and unfinished nodes are absent from the public graph
- visitors can enter LearnGraph or begin a conversation with the team
- product descriptions match current behaviour
- named people, relationships, cases and quotations are approved
- the graph and editorial display work on desktop and mobile
- no editorial workbench language appears in the public experience
- legal and utility routes remain reachable

## Step 9: Complete Origin only if the history arrives

Goal: show transformation, not company chronology.

- [ ] Collect four to six moments when LearnGraph changed in meaning, reach or reality.
- [ ] Record what happened, roughly when, who was involved and why it mattered.
- [ ] Attach an artifact where one survives.
- [ ] Exclude incorporation dates, releases and milestones that changed nothing for users or collaborators.

Exit condition: the node contains a real trajectory. If the material does not arrive, keep Origin unpublished.

## Step 10: Resolve the LG root composition

Goal: make the root click reveal the project rather than repeat its children.

- [ ] Review the current three fragments after the launch nodes are stable.
- [ ] Replace any sentence that could belong to another learning platform.
- [ ] Decide whether source breadcrumbs add meaning.
- [ ] Confirm that the composition has one collective effect despite containing different voices.
- [ ] Remove root content entirely if the composition does not earn the click.

Exit condition: returning to LG feels like returning to the centre of the system, not opening a quotation sampler.

## Step 11: Final launch audit

- [ ] Apply the Door Test to every visible click.
- [ ] Check title, subtitle and internal heading hierarchy across all layouts.
- [ ] Check spacing and endings across all nodes.
- [ ] Test keyboard navigation and focus states.
- [ ] Test routes, refreshes, browser history and direct links.
- [ ] Test desktop, narrow desktop, tablet and mobile.
- [ ] Check reduced-motion behaviour.
- [ ] Confirm external links and legal routes.
- [ ] Search for placeholders, workbench labels, unsupported claims and encoding errors.
- [ ] Run tests, type checking and the production build.

Exit condition: the launch projection is coherent, factual, usable and free of unfinished doors.

## Reserved after launch

These remain in the architecture without a launch promise:

- Research / Open Source
- Open core
- Interoperability
- Research questions
- Contribute
- service topics that fail the workshop test
- any conditional node whose facts, permission or media do not arrive

## Material no longer in the active content queue

- unsupported legacy metrics
- repeated audience packaging
- generic benefit blocks
- generic Research copy
- unverified open-core and self-hosting promises
- European project acronyms used as prestige
- consortium participation presented as research evidence
- university names presented as technical evidence
- generic automation language
- partner descriptions without a verified relationship
- feature inventories without a visitor consequence
